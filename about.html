Heritage & Material Science

The Architecture of Wet-Weather Mastery

Cycling Raincoat was established to dismantle the historic compromise in foul-weather cycling: either suffocating inside an impermeable rubberized poncho, or freezing as road spray breaches water-resistant windbreakers. Our atelier unites alpine mountaineering membrane technology with aerodynamic cycling biomechanics.

1. The Hydrostatic Imperative

When descending a mountain pass at sixty kilometers per hour in torrential rainfall, raindrops strike cycling garments with immense kinetic force. Standard raincoats rated for 5,000mm or 10,000mm hydrostatic head fail instantly under dynamic pressure. We mandate a baseline hydrostatic threshold of 25,000mm across all bespoke outer shells.

2. Vapor Diffusion & Active Venting

A cyclist working at threshold wattage produces upwards of one liter of perspiration every sixty minutes. Without extreme microporous breathability, internal condensation chills the rider to hypothermic levels. Our microporous ePTFE membranes feature billions of microscopic pores per square centimeter—each 20,000 times smaller than a water drop.

Manhattan Bench Craftsmanship

From our flagship salon at 181 Mercer Street in New York, our resident patternmakers craft bespoke raincoats tailored to each rider's unique pelvic tilt, handlebar reach, and shoulder width. By analyzing the rider's static and dynamic postures on a precision stationary rig, we eliminate fabric billowing, flapping, and water pooling across the chest.
