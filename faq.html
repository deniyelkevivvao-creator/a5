Outerwear Specifications & Care FAQ

Comprehensive engineering clarifications regarding membrane physics, hydrostatic ratings, seam-sealing integrity, and long-term textile maintenance.

A hydrostatic head rating of 25,000mm indicates that a fabric specimen can withstand a vertical column of water twenty-five meters high before water begins penetrating the pores. When cycling at high speeds, raindrops impact garments with dynamic pressure far exceeding static rainfall. Generic jackets rated at 5,000mm or 10,000mm will quickly breach at high speeds, whereas our 25,000mm membranes maintain absolute dryness even on sixty kilometer-per-hour alpine descents.
Traditional needle stitching perforates waterproof membranes with thousands of microscopic holes, each requiring external glue or rubber tape. Ultrasonic seam welding fuses synthetic fabric edges using high-frequency vibrational acoustic waves, creating a hermetic molecular bond without needle holes. We then apply an ultra-narrow 8mm thermoplastic polyurethane tape to reinforce the interior seam line, preventing water ingress while keeping the garment flexible.
Waterproofness prevents liquid droplets from penetrating from the outside, while breathability measures how readily water vapor molecules generated by body heat escape from the inside. Resistance to Evaporative Heat Transfer (RET) quantifies this: an RET rating under 4.0 designates extreme breathability suited for intense aerobic exertion. Our 3-layer cycling raincoats achieve an RET of 2.8 to 3.2, ensuring that moisture vapor escapes rapidly to prevent internal clamminess.
Never use conventional laundry detergents, fabric softeners, or bleach, as surfactant residues clog micropores and destroy hydrophobic surface tension. Always wash your jacket using a technical liquid cleaner designed for waterproof membranes on a gentle cycle at thirty degrees Celsius. After washing, hang dry completely, then tumble dry on low heat for twenty minutes to reactivate the fluorocarbon-free durable water repellent (DWR) surface polymers.
When riding in an aggressive aerodynamic posture on drop handlebars, the rider's pelvis rotates forward, pulling standard jackets up past the waistline. Simultaneously, the rear bicycle tire throws a continuous stream of gritty road water straight toward the lower back and saddle chamois. An extended drop-tail hem ensures that the lower lumbar region remains fully covered, deflecting road spray without bunching around the hips.
